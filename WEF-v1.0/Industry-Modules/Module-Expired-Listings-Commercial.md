## 1. Module Overview & Applicability

This Module applies to real estate agents/brokers whose specialization and primary lead-generation strategy is **re-listing properties that previously failed to sell or lease with another broker** — "expired listings." It is weighted toward **commercial asset classes** (office, retail, industrial, multifamily, medical/mixed-use, and land), reflecting both the mgrcre.com reference model and the user's explicit request, but the same diagnostic-first, transparency-led positioning pattern applies with light adaptation to a residential-only or mixed-practice expired-listing specialist — where the client is residential-only, substitute a CMA for a BOV and residential portals (Zillow, Realtor.com, MLS syndication) for the commercial listing services in Section 7.

**Explicitly distinguished from adjacent Modules:**

- **Real Estate Module** — a generalist agent/brokerage without an expired-listing specialization. Use this Module instead specifically when the expired-listing niche is the client's primary positioning and lead source, as in the mgrcre.com pattern, rather than one tactic among many a generalist agent uses.
- **Distressed Property Advocate Module** — an expired listing is not necessarily a *distressed owner* situation (the property simply didn't sell, often due to price or marketing execution, not financial distress); do not assume the personas or compliance sensitivities of that Module apply here unless the specific owner is independently in financial distress as well, in which case a blend is appropriate.
- **Commercial vs. residential weighting is a Charter-level decision.** The Project Charter should state explicitly whether the engagement is commercial-only (the default assumption of this Module's examples), residential-only, or mixed, since the SEO channel strategy (Section 7), valuation tooling (BOV vs. CMA), and Fair Housing applicability (generally inapplicable to purely commercial transactions, fully applicable to any residential component) differ materially.

## 2. Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

- **Real estate broker/agent licensing** — standard state licensing applies; commercial brokerage in most states requires the same license as residential, though a handful of states have distinct commercial licensing or exemption nuances worth confirming.
- **NAR Code of Ethics (or equivalent) — Article 15 and related provisions** — governs make no false or misleading statements about competitors or their business practices. This has unusual relevance in this Module specifically, since the core positioning ("your last broker failed to sell your property — here's why, and here's what we'll do differently") sits directly adjacent to the disparagement line. Content must diagnose *what went wrong* (price, marketing execution, terms, timing) factually and constructively, without asserting the prior broker was incompetent, dishonest, or in violation of any duty, unless that is independently, provably true.
- **Truth-in-advertising / track-record substantiation** — any claim about the client's own historical performance (occupancy rates, portfolio size, average days-on-market improvement, "we relist properties X% faster/at Y% higher price than the original listing") must be accurate and, ideally, sourced from the client's own verifiable transaction history, per the general FTC advertising-substantiation standard referenced throughout this framework's other Modules.
- **Commercial lease and disclosure law** — varies by state and asset type; commercial transactions generally carry lighter mandatory-disclosure burdens than residential (Fair Housing Act protections do not apply to purely commercial property transactions), but state-specific commercial landlord-tenant and environmental disclosure requirements (e.g., known contamination, prior use as a gas station or dry cleaner) still apply and should be confirmed for the client's specific asset classes.
- **Fair Housing Act** — inapplicable to purely commercial transactions, but fully applicable the instant any residential (including multifamily) component is involved; treat multifamily content with the same Fair Housing care as the Real Estate Module.
- **BOV disclosure norms** — a Broker Opinion of Value is, by definition, an informal, broker-authored, non-appraisal estimate; content and BOV deliverables should avoid language that could be mistaken for a certified appraisal, since a BOV "is often performed free of charge by a broker in the hope of earning the owner's business" and is understood in the industry to be inherently less rigorous and more commercially motivated than a licensed appraisal — this distinction should be stated plainly wherever a BOV is offered as a lead-generation tool.

**Known claim-risk language patterns:** disparaging characterizations of the prior listing broker ("they didn't know what they were doing"), unsubstantiated superiority claims about the client's own marketing reach or investor network, and presenting a free BOV as equivalent in rigor to a certified appraisal.

## 3. Persona Library

| Persona | Primary Need | Top Decision Drivers | Top Objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Office Property Owner (Expired Listing) | Re-lease/re-sell in a market with structural vacancy headwinds | A broker with a specific, credible repositioning strategy (not just "we'll try harder") | Skepticism after one failed listing already; concern the market itself, not just the broker, is the problem |
| Retail Property Owner (Expired Listing) | Re-tenant or sell amid shifting consumer/foot-traffic patterns | Broker's specific retail-sector relationships and understanding of changing tenant demand | Fear the asset is simply undesirable at any reasonable price |
| Industrial Property Owner (Expired Listing) | Sell or lease a specialized-use asset with a narrower buyer/tenant pool | Access to a genuinely relevant investor/user database, not a generic marketing blast | Long time-on-market already elapsed; urgency to avoid further carrying costs |
| Multifamily Property Owner (Expired Listing) | Sell an income property that didn't attract a qualified buyer at the prior price/terms | Updated, credible cap-rate and comp analysis; access to actual institutional/private buyer pool | Doubt about whether the original pricing (not the marketing) was the real problem |
| Owner Whose Broker Simply Under-Delivered | Get a broker who will actually execute a marketing plan, not just list and wait | Transparency about the actual plan (channels, reporting cadence, comp process) before committing again | Burned once already; reluctant to sign another listing agreement without proof of a different approach |

## 4. Competitive Landscape Notes

Research-confirmed pattern: expired-listing prospecting is a well-established, high-opportunity niche (recent data cited in research shows a sharp rise in expired listings), and the field splits into (1) **generalist brokers who cold-prospect expired listings opportunistically** as one lead source among several, with no distinct process or public positioning around it; and (2) **dedicated expired-listing specialists** (the mgrcre.com pattern) who build their entire market position around a transparent, diagnostic-first re-listing methodology, often paired with a recurring-revenue property management offer to deepen the client relationship beyond a single transaction. Table stakes: some acknowledgment that "we handle expired listings." Common differentiator gap, directly evidenced by the mgrcre.com model: (a) a documented, shareable process ("here's exactly how we'd remarket your property," delivered before the owner commits, not after) and (b) an honest, specific diagnostic of *why* the prior listing failed (price, marketing execution, terms, buyer/tenant targeting) rather than a generic "let us try" pitch.

## 5. Positioning & Messaging Patterns

Proven angles, directly evidenced: "here's exactly why your listing didn't sell, and here's exactly what we'll do differently" (diagnostic-first, transparency-led); "see our full process before you commit" (the mgrcre.com playbook pattern); "we don't just list — we manage, so your asset keeps performing after the sale falls through or the lease doesn't renew" (property-management cross-sell). Avoid generic "we can sell what your last broker couldn't" positioning with no specific diagnostic or process behind it — and avoid any framing that crosses into disparaging the prior broker (Section 2).

**Flag for compliance:** any characterization of the prior listing broker's performance must stay factual and diagnostic (price was above market, marketing channels were limited, term structure was uncompetitive) rather than character-based (incompetent, dishonest, negligent) absent independently provable fact.

## 6. Information Architecture Patterns

Typical required page types: Home; "Why Your Listing Expired" / diagnostic-methodology page (the core positioning asset — ideally offered as a downloadable playbook or guide, per the mgrcre.com pattern); Asset-class pillar pages (Office, Retail, Industrial, Multifamily, Medical/Mixed-Use, Land); Process/Playbook page (the specific remarketing methodology: comp/cap-rate update, repositioning, multi-channel marketing, reporting cadence); Property Management page (recurring-revenue cross-sell); Current Listings/Inventory; Team/Credentials page (license, years in business, transaction/portfolio metrics); Testimonials; required compliance pages (Privacy Policy, Accessibility Statement, Terms of Use).

## 7. SEO & Keyword Strategy

High-value topical clusters: asset-class-plus-situation terms ("office space relisting [city/region]," "why didn't my commercial listing sell," "industrial property broker [region]"), diagnostic/educational content ("common reasons commercial listings expire," "how a BOV differs from an appraisal," "how cap rates affect commercial pricing"), and asset-class-specific market content. Recommended schema: `RealEstateAgent` or `LocalBusiness` (include license number as identifier), `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`. **Commercial-specific channel note:** unlike residential SEO's reliance on consumer-facing portals (Zillow, Realtor.com), this vertical's actual lead and deal flow depends heavily on **LoopNet, Crexi, and CoStar** listing syndication and on direct outreach to an investor/buyer database — the website's SEO strategy should be understood as building trust and inbound owner-side leads (the "why did my listing expire" searcher), while buyer-side deal flow runs substantially outside the website itself. Do not over-invest website SEO budget chasing buyer-side commercial search volume that these specialized platforms already dominate.

## 8. Trust Signal Requirements

A specific, credible track record (years in business, portfolio size/occupancy metrics, number of relisted properties successfully sold or leased) stated with real, verifiable figures. The diagnostic methodology/playbook made genuinely available before the owner commits (not gated behind an aggressive sales call) — this transparency is itself the single strongest trust signal in this niche, per the mgrcre.com reference model. License number displayed. Direct broker access/availability (the mgrcre.com pattern emphasizes direct principal availability rather than routing through junior staff). Genuine client testimonials, ideally naming the specific asset class and outcome.

## 11. Module-Specific Prompt Library Additions

**Prompt EXP.1 — Non-Disparagement Compliance Scan**

```
Review the attached page copy for [Client Name]'s expired-listing
specialist website. Flag any statement characterizing a prior listing
broker's competence, honesty, or diligence rather than describing
factual, diagnosable reasons a listing may not have sold (price
positioning, marketing channel limitations, term structure, buyer/tenant
targeting). Suggest a factual, non-disparaging rewrite for each flagged
instance, consistent with NAR Code of Ethics (or the applicable local
equivalent) standards on statements about competing brokers.
```

**Prompt EXP.2 — Asset-Class Diagnostic Page Generation**

```
Generate a diagnostic content page for [Client Name]'s expired-listing
specialist website addressing the "[asset class — Office / Retail /
Industrial / Multifamily / Medical / Land]" segment. Structure: (1) the
most common, factual reasons listings expire in this asset class
currently (cite real market dynamics where the client can substantiate
them — do not fabricate market statistics); (2) how [Client Name]'s
diagnostic and remarketing process addresses each; (3) a CTA to request
a free BOV/diagnostic, stated clearly as an informal broker opinion, not
a certified appraisal.
```
