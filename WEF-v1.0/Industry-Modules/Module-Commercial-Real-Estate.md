## 1. Module Overview & Applicability

This Module applies to full-service commercial real estate brokerages offering three service lines — **investment sales** (representing buyers and/or sellers of income-producing commercial property), **owner representation / leasing** (representing landlords in marketing and leasing vacant space, commonly alongside some tenant representation), and **property management** (ongoing operational and financial management of the owner's asset post-lease-up or post-acquisition) — across five asset classes: **retail, office, multifamily, industrial, and land**.

**Explicitly distinguished from adjacent Modules:**

- **Expired Listing Specialist (Commercial-Weighted) Module** — a single-niche specialization (re-listing failed listings) rather than a full three-service-line practice; use that Module instead for a client whose entire positioning is the expired-listing niche, or **blend it as a secondary Module** for a full-service firm (this Module as primary) that also runs an expired-listing-specific lead-generation program.
- **Real Estate Development Module** — a principal taking entitlement/construction/capital risk, not an agency representing third parties or managing their assets. **Blend the two** (Real Estate Development as primary, this Module as secondary) for a developer with an in-house leasing and property-management arm handling its own completed projects.
- **Real Estate Module** — the generalist residential-weighted agent Module; the multifamily asset class in this Module inherits meaningful Fair Housing Act obligations closer to that Module's territory than to pure office/retail/industrial/land commercial practice (Section 2).
- **Home Services Module** — property management includes an ongoing, recurring-service operational dimension (maintenance coordination, tenant relations) with some surface similarity to a local service business, but the client relationship, trust-account regulatory layer, and B2B/asset-owner audience here are different enough that this Module, not Home Services, governs.

## 2. Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

- **Real estate broker/agent licensing** — standard state licensing applies to all three service lines; property management specifically requires particular attention (below).
- **Property management broker licensing and trust accounting.** In most states, any person or company that collects rent or negotiates a lease for compensation on behalf of an owner must operate under a licensed real estate broker, and **must maintain rent and security-deposit funds in a dedicated trust/escrow account, held and reconciled separately from the firm's own operating funds**, under state real estate commission trust-accounting rules. This is a foundational, non-negotiable operational fact that the website's Property Management content should reflect (Section 8) — an owner evaluating a PM firm is, in substantial part, evaluating whether their money will be handled correctly, and stating the trust-account practice plainly is a genuine trust signal, not just a compliance formality.
- **Dual agency and designated agency disclosure.** A firm that represents both a landlord (via owner-rep/leasing) and, in the same or a related transaction, a tenant or buyer, has a disclosable dual-agency situation; many states permit **designated agency** (different licensees within the same firm representing each side, each owing that side full fiduciary duty) as a structural mitigation, but disclosure and client authorization are still required, and research is direct that this does not fully eliminate the underlying tension — "a broker cannot negotiate aggressively for a tenant while simultaneously protecting the landlord's interests." A full-service firm offering both owner-rep/leasing and any tenant-representation service must have a clear, disclosed policy on how this is handled, and website content should not imply undivided loyalty to a party where the firm's actual structure doesn't support that claim.
- **Fair Housing Act — multifamily.** Unlike pure office/retail/industrial/land commercial practice (where the FHA generally does not apply), **the multifamily asset class is squarely covered**: leasing advertising, applicant screening, reasonable-accommodation/service-animal policy, and (for covered new-construction multifamily) accessible design-and-construction requirements all apply. Treat multifamily-specific content with the same Fair Housing care documented in the Real Estate Module.
- **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA).** Where property management includes tenant screening (credit/background checks), FCRA governs how that data may be obtained, used, and disclosed, including adverse-action notice requirements if an application is denied based on a report — this is a service-delivery compliance matter that website content describing the screening process should represent accurately, without overstating what the firm guarantees.
- **Landlord-tenant law and eviction process.** Property management service descriptions should not overstate the firm's role in eviction or other legal proceedings — evictions are a legal process, and PM firms are not law firms; content should describe the firm's coordination role (e.g., "we manage the process and coordinate with counsel") rather than implying the firm itself provides legal representation. Local rent-control and just-cause-eviction ordinances (increasingly common for multifamily in many jurisdictions) should be reflected accurately in any market-specific content.
- **1031 exchange references (investment sales).** "Sell and defer your gain through a 1031 exchange" is a common, legitimate investment-sales marketing angle, but the broker is not a tax advisor — content should present 1031 exchanges as a general concept and direct the reader to their own CPA/qualified intermediary for specifics, mirroring the same unauthorized-advice boundary already established in this framework's Financial Advisor and Distressed Property Advocate Modules.
- **Broker Opinion of Value (BOV) disclosure norms** — identical to the Expired Listing Specialist Module: a BOV is an informal, broker-authored estimate, not a certified appraisal, and should be presented as such.
- **ADA Title III** — applies to retail, office, and other places-of-public-accommodation asset classes; property management and owner-rep content describing accessibility should avoid implying the firm guarantees the owner's ADA compliance, which remains the owner's underlying legal responsibility.
- **CAM (Common Area Maintenance) reconciliation accuracy** — for retail, office, and industrial assets with CAM charge-backs, property management content describing CAM reconciliation should represent the actual methodology accurately, since CAM disputes are a common source of landlord-tenant friction and, occasionally, litigation.

**Known claim-risk language patterns:** guaranteed-occupancy or guaranteed-return claims for property management or investment sales, "we guarantee no vacancy" or similar; implying undivided representation loyalty in a designated-agency structure; presenting a BOV as equivalent to a certified appraisal; presenting 1031-exchange guidance as tax advice.

## 3. Persona Library

| Persona | Primary Need | Top Decision Drivers | Top Objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment Sale Seller (any asset class) | Maximize sale price and certainty of close, often with 1031 timing pressure | Accurate BOV/pricing, buyer-network reach, marketing execution and track record | Fear of leaving money on the table; skepticism about the broker's actual investor network depth |
| Investment Buyer (1031 Exchange or Institutional/Private Capital) | Access deal flow matching their specific buy-box, often under a tight 1031 identification deadline | Broker's off-market and on-market deal flow, underwriting support, cap-rate credibility | Feeling shown everything rather than a curated fit; competing against the broker's own in-house buyers |
| Building Owner Seeking Leasing / Owner Representation | Fill vacant space at market rent with a quality, durable tenant | Broker's tenant network and asset-class-specific expertise, marketing reach, TI/lease-structuring guidance | Broker's actual leasing-velocity track record; commission structure clarity |
| Owner Seeking Property Management | Passive, reliable ownership with protected NOI and minimal owner burden | Transparent reporting, maintenance responsiveness, trust-account integrity, tenant-relations quality | Fear of neglect, poor communication, or hidden fees, often from a past bad PM experience |
| Multifamily Owner (Leasing and/or Management) | Fair-Housing-compliant leasing and management, optimized rent roll | Compliant screening/leasing process, local rent-control/ordinance navigation, occupancy performance | Liability exposure if the PM firm mishandles Fair Housing or screening compliance |
| Tenant Seeking Space (secondary persona) | Find space matching operational needs on favorable terms | Broker's market knowledge and negotiating leverage | Concern about the broker's simultaneous landlord relationships in the same submarket (Sec. 2) |

## 4. Competitive Landscape Notes

Three archetypes: (1) **national full-service firms** (the CBRE/JLL/Cushman & Wakefield/Marcus & Millichap tier) dominating institutional-scale deal flow and head-term search visibility, but often perceived as impersonal for smaller, local, or private-capital deals; (2) **boutique local/regional full-service firms** — this Module's likely client profile — with strong local relationships but frequently thin website content, weak topical SEO depth, and dated listing presentation; (3) **specialist single-service-line firms** (pure investment-sales boutiques, pure tenant-rep shops, pure independent PM companies) with narrower but deeper positioning in one line. Table stakes: a listings page, a "services" page naming the three lines, some agent bios. Common differentiator gap: most full-service firms **list** their three service lines without ever connecting them into a coherent client value proposition ("we sell it, lease it, and manage it — one relationship across your asset's entire lifecycle"), which is the single clearest, most legitimate differentiator available to a genuinely full-service firm and is consistently under-messaged.

## 5. Positioning & Messaging Patterns

Proven angle, directly following from the competitive gap above: **"one firm across your asset's entire lifecycle — acquisition, leasing, management, and eventual disposition"** — a genuine structural advantage a single-service competitor cannot credibly claim. Asset-class-specific credibility angles reinforce this: "the retail team that still understands your center's tenant-mix dynamics," "the industrial specialists fluent in logistics and last-mile fulfillment demand," "the multifamily team that knows [region]'s rent-control landscape inside and out." Avoid generic "full-service commercial real estate brokerage" positioning with no specific proof of depth in any one service line or asset class — research confirms this is the default, undifferentiated positioning across the competitive set.

**Flag for compliance:** any content implying undivided representation loyalty where the firm's actual dual/designated-agency structure doesn't support that claim (Section 2); any guaranteed-occupancy, guaranteed-return, or "we handle evictions" overstatement in property management messaging; any 1031-exchange content that strays from general education into specific tax advice.

## 6. Information Architecture Patterns

Typical required page types: Home; **Services** as three clear pillars (Investment Sales / Leasing & Owner Representation / Property Management), each with its own hub page; **Asset Class pages** (Retail, Office, Multifamily, Industrial, Land), each cross-referencing all three services as applicable to that asset class; Current Listings/Availabilities (for-sale and for-lease inventory, filterable by asset class and service type); Property Management landing page (owner-facing — reporting cadence, maintenance process, trust-account handling statement, and, where offered, an owner portal); Market Reports/Insights (cap-rate trends, submarket vacancy/absorption data — a strong thought-leadership and AI-search-citation asset for this audience); Team/Credentials (broker license numbers, CCIM/SIOR or equivalent designations); dedicated conversion pages — "Sell Your Property" (investment sales), "Lease Your Space" (owner-rep), "Manage My Property" (PM), and a secondary "Find Space" (tenant-facing); required compliance pages (Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Accessibility Statement, agency-relationship disclosure per Section 2, BOV-vs-appraisal disclaimer).

## 7. SEO & Keyword Strategy

High-value topical clusters: asset-class + service + location terms ("[city] office space for lease," "[city] multifamily investment sales broker," "[city] industrial property management," "[region] retail center for sale," "[city] land for sale commercial"); BOV/valuation education content; market-report and cap-rate-trend content (strong for the investor/owner audience and well-suited to AI-mediated search citation, given its genuinely informational nature); and property-management-specific education content ("what does a commercial property manager do," "how CAM reconciliation works"). Recommended schema: `RealEstateAgent` or `LocalBusiness` (brokerage, include license number as identifier), listing-level schema for individual for-sale/for-lease properties, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`. **Channel-allocation note, consistent with the Expired Listing Specialist Module:** buyer- and tenant-side deal flow runs substantially through LoopNet, Crexi, and CoStar syndication; website SEO investment is best directed at owner-side inbound trust-building (investment-sales sellers, leasing/owner-rep prospects, and PM prospects) and thought-leadership content, not at competing for buyer/tenant search volume those platforms already dominate.

## 8. Trust Signal Requirements

Broker license numbers and professional designations (CCIM, SIOR, or equivalent) displayed on team/credential pages. Specific, verifiable track record by service line and asset class (transaction volume/value for investment sales, square footage leased for owner-rep, portfolio size and occupancy rate for property management). **An explicit, plainly stated trust-account handling practice** for property management — that rent and security deposits are held in a dedicated trust/escrow account separate from firm operating funds, per Section 2 — presented as a genuine trust signal, not buried compliance boilerplate. A transparent PM fee structure. A clear, honest statement of the firm's agency/representation policy (dual, designated, or exclusive) wherever the site discusses representing both landlords and tenants. Genuine testimonials segmented by service line (a seller testimonial reads differently than an owner/landlord testimonial or a PM client testimonial, and should be presented as such). Local market presence (a real, physical local office) where applicable.

## 11. Module-Specific Prompt Library Additions

**Prompt CRE.1 — Agency Disclosure & Representation Accuracy Scan**

```
Review the attached page copy for [Client Name]'s commercial real estate
brokerage website. [Client Name]'s actual representation structure is
[dual agency / designated agency / exclusive representation only — insert
as confirmed by the client]. Flag any statement implying undivided
loyalty to a landlord or tenant that is inconsistent with this actual
structure, and any page discussing both landlord and tenant
representation that lacks an appropriate disclosure of how conflicts are
handled. Route to the Compliance/Standards Liaison for final language
confirmation.
```

**Prompt CRE.2 — Property Management Guarantee-Overstatement Scan**

```
Review the attached property management service description for [Client
Name]. Flag any language implying a guaranteed occupancy rate, guaranteed
return, or guaranteed legal outcome (e.g., regarding evictions), and any
language implying the firm provides legal representation rather than
process coordination. Suggest accurate, appropriately qualified
alternative language, and confirm that a statement about trust-account
handling of rent and security deposits is present somewhere on the page.
```

**Prompt CRE.3 — Asset-Class Service Matrix Page Generation**

```
Generate a content outline for [Client Name]'s "[Asset Class — Retail /
Office / Multifamily / Industrial / Land]" page, addressing all three
service lines (Investment Sales, Leasing & Owner Representation, Property
Management) as they specifically apply to this asset class. Include
asset-class-specific market context (cite only data the client can
verify — do not fabricate cap rates, vacancy rates, or absorption
figures), a track-record proof point per service line if available, and
clear CTAs for each service.
```
