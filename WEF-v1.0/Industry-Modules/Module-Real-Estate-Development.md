## 1. Module Overview & Applicability

This Module applies to real estate development companies and sponsors — the **principals** who acquire land or underutilized property, secure entitlements, arrange capital (debt and often outside equity), oversee design and construction, and then lease-up, sell, or hold the finished asset. It covers residential (for-sale subdivision, condo, build-to-rent), multifamily, commercial (office, retail, industrial), and mixed-use development at a broad, cross-asset-class level.

**Explicitly distinguished from adjacent Modules:**

- **Real Estate Module** — a licensed agent/broker representing a third party in a transaction for a commission; carries none of the principal-level entitlement, construction, or capital-formation risk this Module's client carries. Use this Module instead whenever the client is *building* something, not just marketing/transacting an existing asset.
- **Expired Listing Specialist (Commercial-Weighted) Module** — also a brokerage model; a developer who also brokers third-party listings (uncommon but not unheard of) should blend this Module (primary) with that one (secondary).
- **Cash Home Buyer / Distressed Property Advocate / Probate Real Estate Investor Modules** — these clients acquire-and-hold or acquire-and-resell with minimal-to-moderate rehab, not ground-up construction or major entitlement work. The practical dividing line: if the client's business model depends on **taking a property through the entitlement and/or construction process** (not just renovating or flipping an already-usable structure), this Module applies instead.
- **A future Financial Advisor-adjacent capital-raise-only Module** would be a reasonable narrower alternative for a pure syndication sponsor who does not develop ground-up (i.e., primarily assembles capital to acquire stabilized or lightly value-add assets); this Module assumes the client is an actual developer, for whom capital-raising is one function among several, not the entire business.

## 2. Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

This Module carries a genuinely distinct compliance profile from every other Module in this library, centered on two issues with no close analog elsewhere:

- **SEC Regulation D — general solicitation (Rule 506(b) vs. Rule 506(c)).** Where the client raises outside equity capital through a syndication, fund, or joint-venture structure, **the single most consequential Charter-level decision for the entire website is which Reg D exemption the offering relies on, and this must be confirmed with the client's securities counsel before any Investor/Capital content is drafted, not inferred by the delivery team:**
  - **Rule 506(b)** — no general solicitation or public advertising of the specific offering is permitted; the sponsor may raise from an unlimited number of accredited investors plus up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors, verified only to a "reasonable belief" standard, but **only through pre-existing substantive relationships**. Under a 506(b) election, the public website generally **cannot** post specific deal terms, target returns, or an "invest now" call-to-action for an active raise — doing so risks blowing the exemption and creating unregistered-securities exposure. Content must stay at the level of firm-wide track record, thesis, and an invitation to "get in touch to learn more" that leads to a private, relationship-based conversation, not a public offer.
  - **Rule 506(c)** — permits broad public advertising and solicitation of the specific offering, but **every investor must be accredited, and the issuer must take affirmative, documented steps to verify accredited status** (e.g., reviewing tax returns, bank/brokerage statements, or a third-party verification service) rather than merely accepting a self-certification. Under a 506(c) election, specific deal content and public "invest" CTAs are permissible, but the site's investor-facing flow must incorporate this verification step before any specific offering material is delivered.
  - **State blue-sky law** exemption filings layer on top of the federal Reg D exemption and vary by state — confirm the client's counsel has this covered; it is outside this framework's scope to advise on directly.
  - This determination must be logged as a Decision Register entry at Stage Gate 1 and re-confirmed before every subsequent capital-raise-related content or design decision, since it materially shapes the Information Architecture (Section 6), Positioning (Section 5), and every "Invest" page in the Content Model (Section 9).
- **Interstate Land Sales Full Disclosure Act (ILSA).** ILSA applies whenever land is marketed or offered across state lines — and **a public website is inherently interstate**, meaning any developer marketing 25 or more non-exempt lots online may trigger a federal Registration Statement and Property Report delivery requirement (administered by the CFPB) regardless of intent. Common exemptions exist (e.g., completed residences with a certificate of occupancy, statutory small-project exemptions, certain single-family-residence exemptions tied to municipal improvement/zoning commitments), but **none of them should be assumed to apply without the client's real estate counsel confirming the specific project's exemption status** — this is a strict-liability-adjacent federal disclosure regime, not a stylistic advertising concern.
- **Fair Housing Act — advertising and design-and-construction requirements.** Beyond the standard advertising-content care already required in the Real Estate Module (no steering-adjacent language, imagery review), the FHA's **design-and-construction requirements apply directly to the client's product** for covered multifamily dwellings (generally, buildings with 4+ units designed and constructed for first occupancy after March 1991): accessible entrances, usable common areas, accessible routes, and specific unit-interior accessibility features. This is a construction/design compliance matter more than a website-content matter, but marketing content describing accessibility features must be accurate to what is actually being built.
- **ADA Title III** — applies to commercial/mixed-use developments as places of public accommodation; again, primarily a design/construction matter, but marketing claims about accessibility should be accurate.
- **Entitlement/zoning and environmental review accuracy.** Project pages must state the property's *actual current* entitlement status (e.g., "entitlement application submitted," "approved by Planning Commission," "fully entitled") rather than aspirational or premature status claims — this is both a straightforward truth-in-advertising matter and, for projects still in public hearing processes, a matter that can affect the entitlement process itself if the developer is seen as overstating certainty to the community or to capital partners.
- **Pre-construction marketing accuracy.** Renderings, floorplans, delivery-date estimates, and pricing for units/spaces not yet built must carry standard qualifying disclaimer language (Section 8) and must not be presented as final/guaranteed.
- **General contractor licensing** — applies where the developer self-performs construction rather than engaging a third-party GC; confirm the client's actual structure.
- **Environmental disclosure** — Phase I/II Environmental Site Assessment findings, brownfield status, wetlands/Endangered Species Act exposure are diligence-and-disclosure matters that can surface in marketing content (e.g., a "sustainability" or "site history" page) and should be reviewed for accuracy rather than glossed over.
- **Property management broker licensing and trust accounting — build-to-hold developers (v1.1 addition).** Where the developer's business model includes retaining and self-managing a completed asset (rather than selling or fully exiting at stabilization), the same requirement documented in the Commercial Real Estate Module applies the moment the developer or an affiliated entity begins collecting rent: in most states, any party collecting rent or negotiating leases for compensation must operate under a licensed real estate broker, with tenant rent and security deposits held in a dedicated trust/escrow account separate from operating funds. Confirm at Stage Gate 1 whether the client's model is sell-at-stabilization, build-to-hold-and-self-manage, or build-to-hold-with-third-party-management, since this determines whether this requirement applies to the client directly.
- **Fair Housing Act — leasing and advertising, not only design-and-construction (v1.1 addition, sharpening the FHA bullet above).** For any multifamily project the developer leases up itself (as opposed to selling to a third-party operator at completion), Fair Housing obligations extend well beyond the design-and-construction requirements already noted: leasing advertising, applicant screening criteria, and reasonable-accommodation/service-animal policy are all directly covered, exactly as documented in the Commercial Real Estate Module. Treat a developer's own lease-up marketing and leasing-office practices with the same care as a dedicated property manager's.
- **CAM (Common Area Maintenance) reconciliation accuracy — retail/office/industrial build-to-hold (v1.1 addition).** Where the developer retains and leases a retail, office, or industrial asset with CAM charge-backs to tenants, marketing and owner-facing content describing CAM reconciliation should represent the actual methodology accurately — a common source of landlord-tenant friction if misstated.
- **1031 exchange references — dispositions to investors (v1.1 addition).** Where the developer sells a completed or stabilized asset to a capital partner or on the open market and references the buyer's ability to defer gain via a 1031 exchange, present this as a general concept only and direct the reader to their own CPA/qualified intermediary — the developer is not a tax advisor, mirroring the same boundary already established in this framework's Financial Advisor, Distressed Property Advocate, and Commercial Real Estate Modules.

**Known claim-risk language patterns:** specific projected returns, IRRs, or "invest now" language published without regard to the client's 506(b)/506(c) election (Section 2 above); land/lot marketing implying a completed or guaranteed sale without addressing ILSA applicability; entitlement-status overstatement; renderings or delivery-date claims presented as final without qualifying language.

## 3. Persona Library

| Persona | Primary Need | Top Decision Drivers | Top Objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Partner / Investor (LP, family office, JV equity) | Deploy capital into a risk-adjusted development opportunity | Sponsor track record and specific completed-project evidence, alignment of interest (sponsor co-investment), reporting transparency | Illiquidity, an unproven or opaque sponsor, unclear fee/promote structure |
| Landowner / Land Seller | Sell raw or underutilized land or an assemblage parcel | Fair value, certainty that the developer can actually close (financing and entitlement track record), a clear process | Fear of a long due-diligence/entitlement-contingent contract that never closes; fear of being lowballed |
| Prospective Tenant (Office/Retail/Industrial) | Secure space that fits their operational needs on a reliable timeline | Space specs, delivery-date reliability, TI allowance, location/demographics | Construction delay risk; developer's track record of delivering on time |
| Prospective Buyer (For-Sale Residential/Condo) | Purchase a unit that matches their needs, often pre-construction | Value, amenities, builder reputation and construction quality, delivery-date and pricing clarity | Pre-construction price/delivery-date uncertainty; construction-quality risk; deposit security |
| Municipal Stakeholder / Community Member | Understand and weigh in on a project affecting their neighborhood | Transparency about project scope and community impact (traffic, density, jobs, tax base, affordable-housing set-asides) | Distrust of developer motives; concern about neighborhood character change |
| Broker (Capital, Tenant, or Land) | Efficiently access deal materials on behalf of their own client | Fast access to offering materials/site plans, clear co-broker/commission policy, responsiveness | Unclear or unfavorable co-broker terms; slow developer response times |

## 4. Competitive Landscape Notes

Three archetypes, consistent with the researched investment-manager website-structure framework: (1) **large institutional developers** with polished portfolio presentation but generic, low-differentiation "About" and thought-leadership content; (2) **boutique/regional developers** with strong local relationships and story but thin, underdeveloped investor-relations content and weak organic search presence; (3) **vertically integrated operator-developers** who differentiate on execution and in-house control (design, construction, and asset management all under one roof) — these tend to perform best with a portfolio-forward site structure that "surfaces real projects early to contextualize strategy in practice." Common gap, directly evidenced by research: most developer sites either **under-invest in investor-relations content** (no clear track record metrics, no reporting-cadence description, leaving capital partners to rely entirely on offline relationships) or, at the other extreme, **over-solicit in ways that create Regulation D compliance exposure** by publishing specific deal terms without regard to their offering's actual exemption basis. A second consistent gap: community/municipal stakeholders are almost universally left out of the website entirely, handled only through in-person hearings — a missed trust-building opportunity, especially for entitlement-stage projects.

## 5. Positioning & Messaging Patterns

Three proven structural positioning approaches, directly evidenced by research, chosen based on the client's actual differentiation:

- **Thesis-forward** — "we build around a specific, provable market thesis" — appropriate for a sector-specialized or emerging sponsor whose edge is proprietary market insight rather than sheer execution scale.
- **Portfolio-forward** — "judge us by what we've actually built" — appropriate for an execution-focused, vertically integrated operator; leads with real project case studies (asset cards, abbreviated business plans, standardized metrics) rather than abstract philosophy.
- **Platform-forward** — "here is our full platform — teams, capabilities, product vehicles" — appropriate for a multi-strategy manager serving both institutional and wealth-management capital channels; functions more as a directory than a narrative.

Avoid generic "premier developer of quality real estate in desirable locations" positioning, which research confirms is a widespread, undifferentiated default across this competitive set.

**"Dirt to disposition" positioning for vertically integrated, build-to-hold developers (v1.1 addition).** Where the developer also leases up and/or manages its own completed assets (rather than exiting immediately at stabilization), the Commercial Real Estate Module's "one firm across your asset's entire lifecycle" positioning insight extends naturally and even more powerfully here — a developer that controls acquisition, entitlement, construction, lease-up, and ongoing management under one roof can credibly claim a "from dirt to disposition" narrative that neither a pure developer nor a pure brokerage can match. Use this only where genuinely true of the client's actual capabilities; do not apply it to a developer who sells at stabilization and has no in-house leasing or management function.

**Flag for compliance, with the highest weight in this Module:** any specific projected return, IRR, or active-offering solicitation language must be checked against the client's confirmed Reg D election (Section 2) before publication — this is not a stylistic judgment call but a determination requiring the client's securities counsel. Entitlement-status language and pre-construction delivery/pricing claims must be accurate and appropriately qualified (Section 8).

## 6. Information Architecture Patterns

Structure the site around the client's chosen positioning approach (Section 5), but at minimum include: Home; Our Approach/Thesis (if thesis-forward); Portfolio/Projects (current, completed, and pipeline, with standardized metrics per project — the portfolio-forward core asset); Team/Platform/Capabilities (in-house development, construction, and asset-management capabilities, if platform-forward or vertically integrated); **Capital/Investors** (structured strictly per the Section 2 Reg D determination — either a relationship-gated "contact us to learn more" page under a 506(b) election, or a verification-gated specific-offering page under a 506(c) election — never a generic public "invest now" page absent that determination); For Landowners (sell your site to us); Leasing/Availabilities (for tenant-facing commercial projects) or For Buyers (for-sale residential/condo product); **Community/Entitlement** (project-specific pages addressing scope, public hearing schedule/status, and community-impact information — the researched, commonly missing trust-building opportunity); News/Insights (thought leadership); required compliance pages (Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Accessibility Statement, rendering/pre-construction disclaimer, ILSA Property Report reference where applicable, Reg D-appropriate offering disclaimers).

**Multi-project microsite pattern:** for a major individual project (a large mixed-use development, a significant condo project), a dedicated project-specific microsite/subdomain — separate from the corporate site — is a common and reasonable pattern, particularly for leasing or pre-sale marketing; note this as a Charter-level architecture decision rather than a default.

**Post-completion Leasing/Property Management section for build-to-hold developers (v1.1 addition).** Where the client retains and self-manages completed assets, add a Leasing/Availabilities hub (for active lease-up) and a Property Management hub (for stabilized, retained assets) structured per the Commercial Real Estate Module's Section 6 and Section 9 patterns — including the trust-account handling statement, reporting cadence, and (if applicable) owner-portal reference — rather than treating post-completion operations as an afterthought on a general "Portfolio" page. Where this content is substantial enough to be its own service line rather than a light add-on, blend the Commercial Real Estate Module in as a secondary Module per that Module's own Section 1 guidance.

## 7. SEO & Keyword Strategy

At this broad, foundational level: high-value clusters include location-plus-asset-type development terms ("[city] mixed-use development," "[submarket] land for sale to developers"), thought-leadership/thesis content supporting the chosen positioning approach, and project-specific leasing/pre-sale terms for active projects. Recommended schema: `Organization` (corporate site), `RealEstateListing` or `ApartmentComplex`/`Product`-adjacent types for specific for-sale or for-lease projects as applicable, `FAQPage` for community/entitlement pages, `BreadcrumbList`. **Future niche sub-modules should extend this section with asset-class-specific keyword and schema depth** (e.g., multifamily lease-up SEO, land/subdivision SEO, hospitality pre-opening SEO) rather than this Module attempting that depth broadly.

## 8. Trust Signal Requirements

A verifiable track record with specific, standardized project metrics (units delivered, square footage developed, total capitalization, and — only where Section 2's Reg D determination permits public disclosure — realized return metrics). Real team bios with actual credentials and experience. Case studies with real project photography and honest timelines (including any past project that ran long or over budget, if the client is willing to discuss it — a level of transparency research associates with stronger investor trust). A clearly stated investor reporting cadence and methodology. Accurate, current entitlement-status statements on every active project page. Standard pre-construction disclaimer language on every rendering and delivery-date/pricing claim: renderings are an artist's conception based on preliminary plans, not to scale, and subject to change without notice; unit/space availability, pricing, and delivery timing are estimates subject to change. Community-facing transparency (project scope, hearing schedule, impact information) on entitlement-stage projects. **For build-to-hold developers (v1.1 addition):** the same trust-account handling statement, BOV-vs-appraisal distinction (where the developer's in-house team provides valuation opinions to capital partners or landowners), and CCIM/SIOR or equivalent professional designations documented as trust signals in the Commercial Real Estate Module apply directly and should be displayed with the same prominence wherever the developer's in-house leasing or asset-management capability is presented.

## 11. Module-Specific Prompt Library Additions

**Prompt REDEV.1 — Regulation D General Solicitation Compliance Scan**

```
Review the attached page copy for [Client Name]'s real estate development
website. [Client Name]'s current outside-capital offering(s) rely on
Regulation D Rule [506(b) / 506(c) — insert as confirmed by the client's
securities counsel]. Flag any content that: (a) under a 506(b) election,
could constitute general solicitation of a specific active offering
(specific return figures, "invest now" CTAs, public deal-term
disclosure) rather than firm-level track record and thesis content;
(b) under a 506(c) election, is not appropriately gated behind an
accredited-investor verification step before specific offering material
is delivered. Route every flagged instance to the client's securities
counsel for final confirmation — do not conclude compliance yourself.
```

**Prompt REDEV.2 — Entitlement Status Accuracy Check**

```
Review the attached project page copy for [Client Name]'s development
website. Confirm that every statement about the project's entitlement
status (e.g., "approved," "entitled," "in review," "submitted") matches
the actual current status as confirmed by the client's development team
— do not infer or assume a more advanced status than what is explicitly
provided. Flag any ambiguous or potentially premature status claim for
client confirmation before publication.
```

**Prompt REDEV.3 — Pre-Construction Disclaimer Insertion**

```
Review the attached page(s) containing renderings, floorplans, unit/
space pricing, or delivery-date estimates for [Client Name]'s
pre-construction or under-construction project. Confirm that standard
qualifying disclaimer language is present and clearly associated with
each rendering and each pricing/delivery-date claim (artist's conception,
not to scale, subject to change without notice). Flag any page missing
this language.
```
