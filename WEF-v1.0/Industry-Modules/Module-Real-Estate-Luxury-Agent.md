## 1. Module Overview & Applicability

This Module applies to **individual luxury and move-up-focused real estate agents or small teams** marketing residential buyer and seller representation in a defined metro area, whose competitive strategy is built around **owned organic-SEO content and editorial visual authority** rather than reliance on third-party portal visibility (Zillow, Realtor.com, or paid lead-generation platforms). It is a direct specialization of the base Real Estate Module, re-weighted for the luxury/move-up segment specifically.

**Explicitly distinguished from adjacent Modules:**

- **Real Estate Module (base)** — a generalist brokerage or team site where IDX search breadth and lead-capture volume are the primary competitive lever. Use this Module instead when the client's positioning depends on hyperlocal content authority, editorial design restraint, and a self-sufficient content engine as the primary differentiator, as evidenced by the Trillion/Jade Mills/Carolwood/Ginger Martin reference pattern — not on portal-driven lead volume.
- **Real Estate Development Module** — applies to clients taking entitlement, construction, and capital-formation risk (developers/builders), not to agents representing buyers/sellers of existing luxury inventory. Do not blend unless the client is genuinely both an agent and a developer/builder.
- **Commercial Real Estate Module** — applies to income-producing commercial asset classes, not residential luxury resale/listing representation.

**Blend commonly with:** Mortgage Lending Module (secondary, where the agent/team has an in-house or closely affiliated lending relationship requiring an Affiliated Business Arrangement disclosure per Section 2).

## 2. Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

- **State real estate licensing rules** — as in the base Real Estate Module, license number display requirements are near-universal and typically the single most consistently enforced compliance item across states; this Module treats it as non-negotiable, displayed on **every** agent/team bio page and in the site-wide footer, not merely on a single "About" page.
- **Fair Housing Act** — the dominant compliance constraint on all neighborhood/community content, exactly as in the base Module, but with elevated stakes in this Module specifically because hyperlocal narrative content (walkability, lifestyle framing, school-adjacent language) is the core SEO differentiator (Section 7) and therefore produced at higher volume than a generalist site's typically sparse neighborhood pages — more narrative content means more surface area for steering-adjacent language to appear, and a standing content-governance step (Section 11, Prompt LUX.1) is required before publishing any such page.
- **MLS/IDX rules of the local board** — govern display, syndication, and attribution of listing data; the required MLS/IDX attribution statement must appear on every IDX-fed search result and property page.
- **NAR/state association advertising rules** — brokerage-of-record display, "Realtor" trademark usage where applicable, and team-name display rules.
- **RESPA / Affiliated Business Arrangement disclosure** — required wherever the agent/team has a financial relationship with an affiliated lending, title, escrow, or insurance provider referenced or linked from the site.
- **Truth-in-advertising / ranking-claim substantiation** — any "top producer," ranking, or sales-volume claim (a recurring feature of luxury-agent positioning, per the Jade Mills reference pattern) must cite a verifiable, current, MLS-sourced figure rather than a self-reported or undated statistic. This is a materially higher-risk area in the luxury segment than in the general Real Estate Module, because luxury positioning leans harder on production/ranking claims as a trust signal (Section 8) — the temptation to overstate is correspondingly higher, and this Module treats it as a standing QA checklist item (Section 10, SG11).

**Known claim-risk language patterns:** neighborhood/lifestyle copy that could read as steering (demographic-coded phrasing, "family-friendly"/"up-and-coming" framing tied to who a neighborhood is "for"); unverified or stale "top producer"/"#1 agent"/ranking claims; press-mention or "as featured in" credibility strips that overstate the nature of the mention (a paid placement or minor local blurb presented as if it were significant editorial coverage).

## 3. Persona Library

| Persona | Primary Need | Top Decision Drivers | Top Objections |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-Time Buyer | Guided entry into an unfamiliar, high-stakes process | Patient, trustworthy education; clear step-by-step process content | Overwhelm — not knowing what they don't know |
| Relocation Buyer | Confident purchase in an unfamiliar metro, often time-compressed | Deep, verifiable hyperlocal knowledge; ability to build trust remotely (virtual tours, video, responsive communication) | Distance/unfamiliarity — inability to independently verify neighborhood claims from afar |
| Move-Up/Move-Down Seller-Buyer | Coordinated simultaneous sale and purchase | Accurate pricing on both transactions; an agent who manages timing risk explicitly | Timing risk — fear of being caught between a completed sale and an unsecured purchase (or vice versa) |
| Investor Buyer | Data-driven acquisition decisions | Rent comps, appreciation trends, cap-rate-adjacent analysis; agent credibility on investment fundamentals, not just showings | Lack of investment data — generalist agents typically cannot speak credibly to numbers beyond the listing price |
| Luxury Seller | Maximum, discreet exposure for a high-value, distinctive property | Marketing sophistication (professional photography/video, editorial presentation), agent's verifiable luxury track record | Generic marketing treatment — fear the property will be presented with the same template used for a starter home |

## 4. Competitive Landscape Notes

Direct research against the four reference sites named by the client confirms a consistent pattern at the top of the luxury-agent segment: sites cluster around (1) **portal-dependent generalist sites** with strong IDX search but thin, templated agent bios and little to no original content — the pattern the base Real Estate Module treats as the default; and (2) a smaller set of **owned-content, editorial-authority sites** (the Trillion/Jade Mills/Carolwood/Ginger Martin pattern) that invest in original hyperlocal blog content published under their own domain, minimalist photography-forward design, and explicit credibility signaling (press strips, testimonial carousels, segmented listing inventories by status). Table stakes at this tier: IDX/MLS search, agent bio with license number, professional photography. The differentiator gap evidenced consistently across all four reference sites, and the single highest-priority gap to close: **genuine, ongoing hyperlocal content depth** (per-neighborhood pages with real market data, not a single static "communities we serve" list) combined with **design restraint** — competitors at this tier frequently over-design (heavy graphic treatment, stock luxury imagery) where the reference pattern instead uses generous white space and let the photography and data carry the page.

## 5. Positioning & Messaging Patterns

Proven angle, directly evidenced by the reference pattern: **"the neighborhood specialist who knows [target metro] better than anyone,"** supported concretely by hyperlocal content depth rather than asserted as a slogan. Avoid generic "your trusted real estate partner" positioning — it is both the base Module's standing guidance and, per the competitive research above, the exact language pattern the weakest competitor sites default to. Supporting angles by persona (Section 3): "guided, patient support for your first purchase" (First-Time Buyer); "hyperlocal expertise you can trust from anywhere" (Relocation Buyer); "we manage the timing risk so you're never caught between two transactions" (Move-Up/Move-Down); "data-driven representation for investment decisions" (Investor Buyer); "marketing sophistication built for a distinctive property" (Luxury Seller).

**Flag for compliance:** any "top producer," ranking, or production-volume claim used to support the positioning above must be sourced per Section 2 before publication — a common failure mode at this tier is pairing strong positioning language with an unverifiable or stale statistic.

## 6. Information Architecture Patterns

Required page types, directly modeled on the reference-site structural pattern:

- **Home** — hero with headline positioning statement; press/credibility strip ("as featured in," modeled on the Jade Mills pattern); featured/exclusive listings grid; client testimonial carousel (modeled on the Trillion homepage pattern).
- **Buy** — IDX/MLS search tool plus a buyer-process step-by-step guide with an embedded FAQ block.
- **Sell** — home valuation tool plus a seller-process step-by-step guide.
- **Neighborhood/Community pages** (one per target city/area) — the single highest-priority page type for SEO (Section 7); market trend data, school data, walkability/lifestyle narrative, featured listings, directly modeled on the Ginger Martin per-city page and Trillion's community-page pattern.
- **Agent/Team Profile** — photo, license number, bio, specialties, service area, testimonials, active listings; styled with the brand-story minimalism of the Carolwood reference pattern.
- **Listings (individual property pages)** — MLS-fed data, photography/video, virtual tour embed, Fair Housing-compliant descriptions, segmented by status (For Sale / Sold / Leases / Exclusive Off-Market), mirroring the Jade Mills and Ginger Martin listing-segmentation pattern.
- **Resource/Blog Hub** — ongoing original articles (investment guides, buyer/seller education, hyperlocal market updates), published under the site's own domain rather than syndicated elsewhere, modeled on Trillion's active blog.
- **Compliance pages** — Fair Housing statement, Privacy Policy, Accessibility Statement, brokerage-of-record disclosure, Affiliated Business Arrangement disclosure where applicable.

## 7. SEO & Keyword Strategy

High-value topical clusters, directly modeled on the reference pattern: **"[neighborhood] homes for sale,"** **"[neighborhood] real estate market trends,"** **"relocating to [city] guide,"** **"first-time home buyer [city]."** This Module treats the Neighborhood/Community page cluster as the primary organic-SEO engine of the entire site (per the Trillion "owned content over portal dependence" model) rather than a secondary content layer — the site's SEO strategy should not depend on third-party portal visibility. Recommended schema: `RealEstateAgent`/`RealEstateListing` on property pages, `LocalBusiness` for the brokerage, `Person` (with license number as identifier) for agent bios, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`. **Canonicalization note:** apply careful canonical tagging on IDX-fed listing pages, since syndicated MLS data appears near-identically across many competing sites in the same market and unmanaged duplication of that data is a known thin/duplicate-content penalty risk (as in the base Real Estate Module, elevated here by this Module's greater reliance on organic search as the primary lead channel).

## 8. Trust Signal Requirements

Agent/broker license number visible on every bio page and in the site-wide footer (Section 2). Brokerage-of-record branding per local MLS/association rules. **Press/local-media credibility strip** on the homepage (the Jade Mills pattern) — every mention displayed must be substantiated and not overstated (Section 2). **Permissioned client testimonials with closed-transaction proof points**, not generic praise quotes. **Professional photography of both the agent and the properties throughout** — this Module treats visual trust signaling as higher-stakes than in the base Real Estate Module, since the reference competitive set (Section 4) differentiates specifically on photography-forward, editorial presentation rather than volume of listings shown. Verifiable, current, MLS-sourced production statistics where any ranking/volume claim is made (Section 2).

## 11. Module-Specific Prompt Library Additions

**Prompt LUX.1 — Fair Housing Compliance Scan (Neighborhood/Community Content)**

```
Review the attached Neighborhood/Community page copy for [Client Name]'s
luxury real estate website for any language that could be read as
steering under the Fair Housing Act — including demographic-coded
phrasing, implications about who the neighborhood is "for," or
"family-friendly"/"up-and-coming" framing that correlates with
protected-class composition. Flag specific phrases and propose neutral,
factual replacements grounded in walkability, commute times, amenity
proximity, and verified market data — do not assert replacement language
as final; route flagged items to the Compliance/Standards Liaison before
publication.
```

**Prompt LUX.2 — Production/Ranking Claim Substantiation Check**

```
Review the attached page copy for [Client Name]'s luxury real estate
website for any "top producer," ranking, "#1 agent," or sales-volume
claim. For each instance, confirm it cites a specific, current,
verifiable, MLS-sourced figure with an identifiable time period and
geography. Flag any claim that is undated, unsourced, self-reported
without independent verification, or based on a data set narrower than
what the claim implies (e.g., a neighborhood-level ranking presented as
a metro-wide ranking). Recommend either the correctly scoped claim or
removal.
```

**Prompt LUX.3 — Neighborhood/Community Page Generation**

```
Generate a Neighborhood/Community page for [Client Name]'s luxury real
estate website covering [neighborhood/area name] within [target metro].
Structure: (1) a factual market-trend summary (do not fabricate
statistics — flag any figure the client must supply or verify); (2)
walkability, commute-time, and amenity-proximity narrative in place of
subjective lifestyle framing; (3) school data presented factually
(ratings/programs, not quality judgments tied to demographic
composition); (4) a featured-listings block for the area; (5) an agent
CTA. Route the completed draft through Prompt LUX.1 before publication.
```
